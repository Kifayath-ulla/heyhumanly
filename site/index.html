How it works

Not just rephrased. Explained.

Other tools rewrite your text and leave you guessing. HeyHumanly shows you why, so you improve.

01

Write it your way

Paste a message, dictate it, or type in any text box on the web. Slang and casual are fine.

02

See a lighter edit

Your tone and energy stay. Only filler, repeats and extra words are trimmed, with your own words highlighted.

03

Learn the habit

Each change names the habit, quotes you, links the research behind the tip and tells you what to do next time.

Built to teach

More than a spellchecker.

Your voice

Keeps your tone

Casual stays casual. HeyHumanly reads your register first, then makes small cuts only. It never turns “yo gang” into a corporate email.

The evidence

Tips with sources

Concise text, readable wording and hedging each link to the study behind the tip. Where the research is mixed, it says so.

Style

Wordy phrases

“In order to” becomes “to”. “Due to the fact that” becomes “because”.

Clarity

Passive and repeats

Flags hidden doers and three sentences in a row that start the same way.

Progress

Your habits over time

Insights shows which habits you repeat, so you work on one thing at a time.

Install the Mac app

Ready in about five minutes.

Most of that is the one-time model download.

  1. Download and open the DMG

    Open HeyHumanly-0.1.5.dmg from your Downloads folder.

  2. Drag HeyHumanly into Applications

    Then open it from Applications.

  3. Set up the local model (once)

    The first-run tour has a Get Ollama button, then a Download button with a progress bar. The model is about 4.7 GB on a 16 GB Mac (1.9 GB on smaller Macs).

  4. Allow Accessibility, then try the example

    macOS asks before any app can read a text box. Allow it in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.

About the first open. Version 0.1.5 is not yet notarized by Apple, so macOS may warn you. See install help, check the checksum on the release page, or build it from source.

Why HeyHumanly was built

A coach, not a correction machine.

Most writing tools fix your sentence and move on, so you make the same mistake tomorrow. HeyHumanly keeps your voice, shows what could be tighter, and tells you why, so the habit changes.

Built for people who write things they would not want sitting on someone else's server.

Zero telemetry

No tracking and no analytics. Your writing stays on your computer.

Open source (MIT)

Free and open. Read every line on GitHub.

No subscription

No account, no paywall, no trial that expires.

Where your writing goes

Your text is checked by a model on your computer. HeyHumanly has no account, analytics or tracking, and sends your text only to the Ollama address in its Settings, which is localhost (this computer) by default.

What it reads

Only the one text box you are typing in, after you pause. Never password fields, password managers, terminals or code editors.

What it stores

Sessions, review cards, your dictionary and settings stay in this app's preferences on your Mac. You can clear them in Settings.

What still uses the internet

Downloading Ollama and the model (done by Ollama and your browser, not by HeyHumanly), and loading this website. HeyHumanly does not check for updates yet.

Read the full privacy page. If you point HeyHumanly at another computer in Settings, it warns you, because then your text leaves this computer.

Honest limits

  • It works well in Slack, Safari and TextEdit, where it was tested. Chrome, Mail, WhatsApp and other apps are not fully tested.
  • In Slack it shows the dot but cannot underline words, because Slack does not share word positions with other apps.
  • Local models are weaker than the biggest cloud models. The 3B model misses some grammar, and Hindi translation is unreliable.
  • Explanations for long rewrites can take 5 to 10 seconds.
  • The research notes cite real studies, but no study proves one rewrite is best for everyone. Where the evidence is mixed, the app says so.
  • Version 0.1.5 is early. There is no automatic update yet: download a new version from this page.

Questions

Is HeyHumanly really free?

Yes. The app and the Chrome extension are MIT-licensed and free to use. The only costs are your own disk space and the time to download the model. There is no account or subscription.

Will it make my message sound formal?

No. HeyHumanly reads your register first. A casual message stays casual, and only filler, repeated words and extra words are trimmed. A "More formal" button exists for the times you want it.

Where do the research notes come from?

Each tip links to the study or report behind it, such as Nielsen Norman Group's web-reading research and Oppenheimer's 2006 paper on plain writing. Where evidence is mixed, for example on leading with your point, the app says so.

Does it work offline?

After Ollama and the model are downloaded, HeyHumanly runs on your computer without internet. Downloading them needs internet once.

Do I need Ollama?

Yes. HeyHumanly asks Ollama to run the model. The first-run tour links to the Ollama download and then downloads the model for you with a progress bar. Already have Ollama? HeyHumanly finds it automatically.

Which model does it use?

Any model in Ollama. On a Mac with 12 GB of memory or more it recommends Qwen 2.5 7B (Apache-2.0 license, about 4.7 GB). On smaller Macs it recommends Qwen 2.5 3B (about 1.9 GB). You can pick any installed model in Settings.

How do I use the Chrome extension?

Install it, allow it to reach Ollama on your computer (one button in the Mac app's Settings, or one Terminal command, explained on the extension page), then select text in any text box and press the shortcut. It shows the edit, the reason and the research, and lets you apply or copy it.

Why does the Mac app need Accessibility permission?

macOS only lets an app read a text box in another app if you allow it. HeyHumanly uses it to read the one box you are typing in, and to paste a fix you accepted. Without it, the coach window still works with text you type or paste into it.

macOS says it cannot verify the app. What do I do?

That is expected for this early version, which is not notarized by Apple. See the install help page for the steps, and verify the checksum first.

Is it a Grammarly alternative?

It does a similar job of marking mistakes, but its main aim is teaching: it keeps your tone, explains the rule, links the research, and runs on a local model instead of a cloud service.

Can I contribute or report a bug?

Yes. The code is on GitHub. Open an issue for bugs. Do not paste private text into public issues.
